## What is BMI?

Body mass index (BMI) compares your weight with your height:

> **BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m) ÷ height (m)**

Take a woman who is 1.65 m tall and weighs 66 kg. Her BMI is 66 ÷ 1.65 ÷ 1.65 = **24.2**.

You can skip the arithmetic and use the [BMI & waist calculator](/tools/bmi-calculator/).

## Is a BMI of 24 normal?

It depends on which chart you use. On the global WHO chart, 24.2 counts as "normal", because that chart treats anything below 25 as normal. On the chart for Asian and Indian adults it counts as **overweight**.

| BMI (kg/m²) | Global WHO category | Category for Indian adults |
|---|---|---|
| Below 18.5 | Underweight | Underweight |
| 18.5 – 22.9 | Normal | Normal |
| 23.0 – 24.9 | Normal | **Overweight** |
| 25.0 – 29.9 | Overweight | **Obesity** |
| 30 and above | Obesity | Obesity |

India's consensus guideline on obesity (2009) adopted these lower cut-offs, following a 2004 WHO expert review of Asian populations.

## Why are the cut-offs lower for Indians?

Two people can have the same BMI and very different bodies. In a well-known comparison, an Indian man and a British man had almost the same BMI, but the Indian man carried a far higher proportion of body fat. Researchers called this the *Y-Y paradox*, after the surnames of the two authors.

Across many studies the pattern is consistent. At the same BMI, South Asians on average:

- carry a higher percentage of body fat,
- store more of it inside the abdomen, around the organs, and
- develop type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure and heart disease at a lower BMI.

A cut-off exists to flag risk early enough to act. For Indians, that point comes sooner.

## What BMI cannot tell you

BMI is cheap and quick, which makes it a good screening number. It is not a diagnosis. It has two blind spots:

1. **It cannot tell fat from muscle.** A wrestler and a sedentary office worker can have the same BMI.
2. **It does not show where the fat is.** Fat inside the abdomen does far more harm than fat under the skin of the hips and thighs.

That is why the next number to check is your waist. See [waist-to-height ratio: the one number worth checking](/learn/waist-to-height-ratio/).

## What should I do with my number?

- **18.5 – 22.9:** keep your current habits and check your waist once a year.
- **23 – 24.9:** a good time to act, because small changes to food, activity and sleep work best now.
- **25 or more:** talk to a doctor about checking blood sugar, blood pressure and cholesterol, and about a plan that fits your life.
