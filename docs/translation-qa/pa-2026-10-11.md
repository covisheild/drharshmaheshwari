## Not changed, and why

- **Thirst** ("if you have thirst", "symptoms such as thirst"): the English says just "thirst", and the Punjabi says just that. The second string has no "without trying" in the English, so none was added; the first keeps "ਬਿਨਾਂ ਕੋਸ਼ਿਸ਼ ਦੇ".
- **"Translated ... and checked by the author"**: the Punjabi line is a faithful translation of that key; the page code picks which label to show (Punjabi uses the "checked" one, not "by the author").
- **IDRS bands "30 to 50: moderate"**: the English says so; scores move in steps of 10.
- **Intensity words**: already one word each, everywhere: moderate ਦਰਮਿਆਨੀ, vigorous ਜ਼ੋਰਦਾਰ, energetic ਫੁਰਤੀਲੀ always followed by (ਦਰਮਿਆਨੀ ਤੋਂ ਜ਼ੋਰਦਾਰ), severe (disease) ਗੰਭੀਰ. ਸਖ਼ਤ appears only where the English says "hard" or "strenuous". Checked by search.
- **"percentile {ordinal}" word order**: the glossary rule (the ordinal placeholder is filled with a plain number, so "ਪਰਸੈਂਟਾਈਲ 3" reads correctly).
- **"WHO has counted every minute since 2020"**: back-translated as "since 2020", which is what the English says.
- **Links to `/pa/...` pages, "(in English)" notes on links to English-only pages, "(The account pages are in English.)"**: by design.
- **ਮੁਫ਼ਤ (free)**: the English says "free" on these support and calculator strings; the no-"free" rule covers book pages only.
- **Wasted / severely wasted (WHO under 5)** use ਪਤਲਾਪਣ, the same word as IAP "thinness"; **"Credits"** is ਧੰਨਵਾਦ ("thanks"); **"accurate"** in the BMI title is ਸਹੀ ("right"); "ਜਲਦੀ" for "earlier" (as loose as the English). Minor, meaning kept; see open questions.
- **ਲਿੰਗ** for sex: the standard word on Punjabi forms. **ਸਰਗਰਮੀ** for activity: formal but understood, and used consistently. English loans (ਸੇਵ, ਡਿਵਾਈਸ, ਰੀਡਿੰਗ) are how Ludhiana phone users speak.
- "ਲੋੜ ਜੋਗੀ ਕਸਰਤ" for ICMR-NIN's "enough exercise" in pregnancy: idiomatic and matches "enough".

## Open questions (decisions, not errors)

1. **ਜ਼ੋਰਦਾਰ (vigorous) and ਫੁਰਤੀਲੀ (energetic)**: the reviewer finds ਜ਼ੋਰਦਾਰ also means "forceful/loud" and ਫੁਰਤੀਲੀ "nimble". Both are glossary terms and consistent; ਫੁਰਤੀਲੀ is always glossed. Change only if a Punjabi reader finds them odd (it would change every activity string).
2. **Thirst**: Hindi and Assamese say "excessive thirst" (ज़्यादा प्यास) as a diabetes symptom; English and Punjabi say just "thirst". Harsh may want "excessive thirst" in the English, then in every language.
3. **WHO "wasted"** is shown with the IAP word for "thinness" (ਪਤਲਾਪਣ). Keep, or use a separate word (e.g. "ਬਹੁਤ ਪਤਲਾ (ਵੇਸਟਡ)").
4. **"Credits" as ਧੰਨਵਾਦ ("thanks")** on the disclaimer page title and its links. Keep, or use ਕ੍ਰੈਡਿਟ / ਸਰੋਤ.
5. **Family history in IDRS** is now "diabetes in the family" (what the score asks about). If Harsh prefers the general term, "ਪਰਿਵਾਰ ਵਿੱਚ ਬਿਮਾਰੀ ਦਾ ਪਿਛੋਕੜ" is the alternative.
